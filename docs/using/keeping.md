# Keeping and sharing

Nothing you draw is uploaded anywhere. That makes keeping and sharing your
decision rather than something that happens automatically, so it is worth two
minutes to know the options.

## While you are working

The planner saves a **draft** of whatever is on screen a couple of seconds after
you stop changing it, in this browser. If the tab crashes or you close it by
accident, you are offered the draft back when you return.

A draft is not a filing system: there is one, it is overwritten constantly, and
it expires after a week. It is there to protect you between decisions, not to
be one.

## Keeping a design

Open **Designs** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>O</kbd>)
and press **Keep this design**. It gets a name you choose, a picture of its own
plan, and a place in a grid you can come back to. From there you can rename,
duplicate and delete.

This is still your browser on your machine. Clearing your browsing data removes
them.

## Saving a file

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> downloads a `.blueprint3d` file —
the whole design, as text, on your disk. Open it again by dropping it on the
planner or through the file button in the top bar.

This is the one that survives everything: a cleared browser, a new laptop, a
different person.

## Sending a design to somebody

There are two ways, and which one you want depends on whether your design uses
anything the other person will not have.

**A link.** <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> makes
one. The entire design is compressed into the link itself — there is no server
holding it, and the part after the `#` is never sent to one. Whoever opens it
sees the design read-only until they keep a copy of their own, so you cannot
have your design edited out from under you.

Very large designs make links too long for some email clients to survive. When
that happens the planner says so and gives you the length, and a file is the
answer.

**A bundle.** A `.zip` carrying the design *and* anything the recipient will not
already have — which today means models you imported yourself. Everything from
the catalog is in their copy of the planner already, so a bundle of catalog
furniture is just the design with a wrapper.

## Getting it out into something else

The export menu in the top bar writes:

- **Wavefront OBJ** and **glTF 2.0**, for another 3D tool
- **A photo** of the 3D view, at up to four times the screen resolution
- **A 360° panorama**, for a viewer or a headset
- **The plan as SVG or PNG**, at a stated scale with a title block
- **A printed plan**, straight to your printer

## Working offline

After your first visit the planner keeps itself on your device, so it opens and
works with no network at all — drawing, furnishing, saving and the whole
catalog you have already looked at.

Some browsers will also offer to **install** it, which gives it its own window
and its own icon. It is the same application either way; nothing is downloaded
that was not downloaded anyway.

When a new version is published you are told, and it is applied when you choose
— never in the middle of what you were doing.
